# AI WORKS Act

## Artificial Intelligence Worker Opportunity, Readiness, Knowledge, and Skills Act

**Working public package — August 5, 2026**  
**Public proposition:** *Make AI work for Americans—not the other way around.*

> Protect the worker. Fund the pathway. Verify the skill. Let the worker own the record. Publish the result. Stop the program if it does not work.

This is a policy architecture, not introduced legislative text and not enacted law. Legal citations, bill statuses, funding assumptions, and program facts require counsel and current-source verification before external legislative use.

---

## 1. The problem

The immediate AI labor-market risk is not a cinematic replacement of every worker. It is a quieter failure of the entry and transition ladders through which people become experienced workers.

Employers can rationally reduce junior hiring, remove routine work, redesign positions, and avoid training costs before aggregate unemployment shows a dramatic shock. The result can be a national capability gap that arrives years after the original hiring rung has weakened.

At the same time, indiscriminate AI access does not automatically create agency. A person can generate large amounts of material without developing durable skill, accepting another person’s standards, maintaining an artifact, or producing something another human being uses.

The public response should therefore be neither prohibition nor indiscriminate acceleration. Public investment should connect AI-era learning to paid work, real practice, portable evidence, privacy, and measurable outcomes.

### The governing test

**Public AI workforce investment must produce verified capability applied to consequential work.**

“Consequential work” does not mean government judges private meaning, art, hobbies, or purpose. It means that advanced publicly funded training must connect a defined skill to an external context: employment, apprenticeship, occupational practice, research, public service, or genuine community use.

## 2. The public bargain

### Rule 1 — Workers come before technology

Public funds may not finance:

- replacement layoffs;
- worker misclassification;
- unpaid productive labor disguised as training;
- surveillance or scoring unrelated to program administration;
- a centralized federal person-level AI skills database.

### Rule 2 — Training must reach real work

Advanced programs must produce evidence that a defined skill was applied under observable conditions and evaluated outside the learner’s own generation session.

Acceptable contexts include paid employment, Registered Apprenticeship, supervised occupational practice, independently evaluated research, public service, and documented community use.

### Rule 3 — The worker owns the record

Any portable Learning and Employment Record must be:

- voluntary;
- selective rather than all-encompassing;
- controlled by the individual;
- exportable in an interoperable format;
- revocable where technically possible;
- separated from a centralized federal worker score.

The federal government may publish aggregate program outcomes. It may not maintain a universal person-level ranking of workers.

### Rule 4 — Use existing institutions

The package routes through Workforce Pell, WIOA, Registered Apprenticeship, state workforce systems, American Job Centers, community colleges, libraries, unions, tribal institutions, employers, and existing federal research capacity.

It does not create a parallel national training bureaucracy.

### Rule 5 — Prove it or stop it

New grant authority terminates five years after the first appropriation. Continuation or expansion requires:

- an independent evaluation;
- published costs and outcomes;
- public reporting of violations and complaints;
- a new affirmative act of Congress.

There is no automatic Scale phase.

## 3. Legislative architecture

## TITLE I — MEASURE WORKPLACE CHANGE

### §101. AI Workforce Research Hub

Codify and strengthen an interagency labor-market measurement function led by the Department of Labor, with appropriate participation from Census, NIST, NSF, Education, Commerce, and other relevant agencies.

The Hub should report:

- occupational and task change;
- entry-level hiring and promotion patterns;
- employer adoption and redesign;
- displacement and reduced-hours indicators;
- wage, geographic, demographic, and firm-size differences;
- demand for apprenticeship and transition pathways.

Reporting should be aggregate and privacy-protecting.

### §102. Employer transition notices

For employers receiving funds under this Act, require disclosure of material workforce reductions, conversion of employees to contractors, reduced hours, or substantial role redesign connected to the funded activity.

The notice is for measurement and enforcement. It does not create a general federal license to deploy or prohibit AI.

### §103. Common definitions

Direct Labor to update annually, through notice and public comment, definitions for:

- AI-affected occupation;
- paid work-based learning;
- verified competency evidence;
- displacement event;
- participating employer;
- publicly funded advanced AI training.

Definitions should remain technology-neutral enough to survive model and tool changes.

---

## TITLE II — PROTECT WORKERS AND PERSONAL RECORDS

### §201. No-displacement condition

A participating employer may not use federal assistance under this Act to directly finance a replacement layoff or equivalent conversion of an employee position into contingent labor.

Enforcement tools include:

- repayment and clawback;
- suspension;
- future ineligibility;
- public notice of a final violation;
- referral under existing labor law where applicable.

This condition is limited to the public funds and program participation. It is not a general prohibition on automation.

### §202. Paid-training and classification rule

Productive work performed for an employer must be paid and properly classified. A learner may not be placed into piecework, bounty work, or uncompensated production merely because the activity is called training.

### §203. Worker-controlled Learning and Employment Records

Support interoperable, voluntary records that allow a worker to carry selected evidence of learning and work.

Prohibit:

- a federal person-level AI competency score;
- mandatory disclosure to employers outside program requirements;
- sale of participant records;
- use for unrelated law-enforcement, immigration, credit, insurance, or benefits determinations without separate lawful authority.

### §204. Data minimization

Collect only information needed to administer, audit, and evaluate the program. Publish aggregate results and apply suppression or other privacy controls to small groups.

---

## TITLE III — FUND PAID TRANSITION PATHWAYS

### §301. Workforce Pell alignment

Use existing short-term Pell authority for qualifying high-skill, high-wage, in-demand programs, subject to strong quality floors.

Provide state capacity support for approval, outcome reporting, articulation to credit where practical, and removal of failing programs.

### §302. WIOA supportive services

Permit and prioritize supportive services that allow workers to complete intensive transition programs, including transportation, child care, tools, connectivity, and time-limited living support under existing authority.

### §303. Registered Apprenticeship

Make Registered Apprenticeship and other paid work-based learning the preferred advanced pathway.

Federal support may reimburse a declining share of wages and related instruction. The employer absorbs a greater share as apprentice productivity rises.

### §304. Local delivery

Eligible delivery partners include:

- state and local workforce boards;
- American Job Centers;
- community and technical colleges;
- Registered Apprenticeship sponsors;
- unions and joint labor-management programs;
- tribal governments and institutions;
- public libraries acting with a qualified training partner;
- nonprofit and employer consortia meeting worker-protection rules.

### §305. Real-Work Standard

An advanced publicly funded credential must include at least one of:

- paid employment or apprenticeship evidence;
- supervised occupational practice;
- independent evaluation by a qualified external reviewer;
- documented adoption or use by a real beneficiary;
- a handoff or maintenance plan for a public or community artifact.

Completion alone is not sufficient evidence of success.

---

## TITLE IV — VERIFY OUTCOMES AND SUNSET

### §401. Public outcome scoreboard

Publish annual aggregate results for each program, provider, and major participant category where privacy permits:

- enrollment and completion;
- paid placement at six and twelve months;
- wage change;
- retention;
- employer co-investment;
- verified real-work evidence;
- cost per sustained positive outcome;
- complaints, displacement findings, privacy violations, and clawbacks.

Software response time, token output, course satisfaction, or raw credential counts may not substitute for human outcomes.

### §402. Independent evaluation

Commission a methodologically independent evaluation with a public report. Where feasible, compare participants with relevant nonparticipants and distinguish short-term placement from durable outcomes.

### §403. Funding ceiling

Authorize no more than **$1.5 billion over five years** in new demonstration funding across the Act, excluding amounts already available under existing programs.

Appropriations remain subject to the normal process. This is not an entitlement and not a self-funding mechanism.

### §404. Sunset and renewal

New grant authority terminates five years after the first appropriation. Agencies may close awards and complete audits, but may not issue new demonstration grants without subsequent legislation.

Congress receives the evaluation at least twelve months before the sunset date.

---

## 4. What the package does not create

AI WORKS does not create:

- a national AI acceleration mandate;
- a federal worker score or social-credit file;
- a universal compute payment;
- an AI services tax;
- a federal model-development or data-center construction program;
- a government-hosted marketplace for unpaid gig bounties;
- automatic continuation after five years;
- government authority to judge private creativity, entertainment, or personal meaning.

## 5. The related working demonstration

The AI WORKS website includes a secondary Public Service Router. It demonstrates the same governing principle in software: narrow the model’s authority, preserve human-authored sources, show evidence, and abstain rather than guess.

The router follows this sequence:

```text
person's words
→ deterministic emergency check
→ local retrieval of reviewed records
→ model verifies one candidate or abstains
→ structured output validation
→ human-authored result and destination disclosure
```

The model may not create a URL, answer the government question, determine eligibility, select an escalation record, or route when two candidates remain plausible.

The U.S. deployment is an experimental directory, not an official government service. The module is released under the Unlicense so another country, public body, or organization may adapt it with its own official-source research, maintenance owner, languages, escalation rules, and society-chosen initiative.

## 6. Implementation sequence

### First 90 days

Actions that can be pursued under existing authority or preparation:

1. establish the interagency measurement work plan;
2. prepare model state guidance for AI-affected occupations and Workforce Pell quality;
3. identify Registered Apprenticeship occupations and sponsors;
4. negotiate worker-protection and reporting terms with labor, states, providers, and employers;
5. publish draft outcome definitions and privacy rules;
6. begin a small record/interoperability pilot without a centralized federal ledger.

### Year 1

- launch limited state and local demonstrations;
- fund paid pathways rather than standalone AI courses;
- start the public outcome scoreboard;
- publish violations and corrective actions;
- expand evaluation sets and audit methods.

### Years 2–4

- remove failing providers;
- shift support toward pathways with durable paid outcomes;
- compare delivery models;
- strengthen privacy and worker-control mechanisms;
- prepare the independent evaluation.

### Year 5

- publish the full evaluation;
- close new grant authority;
- require Congress to choose termination, redesign, or renewal.

## 7. Political case

### Worker and labor case

The package rebuilds paid entry and transition pathways, rejects piecework, protects classification, and conditions public money on worker outcomes.

### State and local case

States retain program approval and delivery roles. Federal funds build capacity and measurement rather than preempting state workforce systems.

### Fiscal case

The demonstration is capped, uses existing infrastructure, publishes cost per sustained outcome, and ends without a new vote.

### Industry case

Employers gain a supported talent pathway and portable evidence framework without a new AI tax, deployment mandate, or federal model standard. Public support comes with enforceable worker conditions.

### Civil-liberties case

There is no centralized person-level Skills Ledger. Records are voluntary and worker-controlled; outcome reporting is aggregate.

## 8. Questions a hostile hearing should ask

**Is this an AI acceleration bill?**  
No. It is a worker-transition demonstration. It does not mandate AI adoption or fund model development.

**Does it subsidize companies that fire people?**  
No. Public assistance carries a no-displacement condition, paid-training rule, clawbacks, and future ineligibility.

**Does it create a federal database of every worker’s capabilities?**  
No. The package prohibits a centralized person-level federal AI skills ledger.

**Why fund short programs when their value is uncertain?**  
Because the uncertainty is real, the program begins as a capped demonstration, quality floors are required, and continuation depends on durable outcomes rather than completion counts.

**Is government deciding what work is meaningful?**  
No. The Real-Work Standard applies only to advanced publicly funded training and asks for external use or evaluation. It does not regulate private life or creativity.

**What happens when it fails?**  
The authority sunsets. Congress must pass another law to continue.

## One-page ask

**The problem:** AI is changing the entry and transition ladders before aggregate unemployment fully shows the damage. Access alone does not produce durable human capability.

**The proposition:** Make AI work for Americans—not the other way around.

**The package:** Measure workplace change; protect workers; fund paid pathways through existing institutions; let workers carry voluntary evidence; publish outcomes; sunset after five years.

**The ceiling:** $1.5 billion over five years in new demonstration authority.

**The prohibition:** No federal worker score, no displacement subsidy, no unpaid productive training, no automatic scale-up.

**The ask:** Build a bipartisan, worker-first demonstration around existing workforce authorities, independent evaluation, and an explicit off switch.
